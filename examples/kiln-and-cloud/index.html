A stoneware studio in Bonfim, Porto

Everyday plates and bowls, thrown by hand in Porto.

Thirteen days from a bag of clay to your table

Most of that time the piece is just waiting: to firm up, to dry, to cool. We don’t rush it, because clay remembers every shortcut and cracks in the kiln.

  1. Day 1

    Wedge

    We knead each 1.2 kg ball of stoneware sixty times to push the air out. An air pocket is a piece that bursts at 900 °C.

  2. Day 1

    Throw

    Centred and pulled up on the wheel in three passes. Every rim is thrown to the same 4 mm, then left to firm overnight.

  3. Days 2–8

    Trim and dry

    Turned upside down to carve the foot ring, then dried slowly under cloth. Porto’s damp winters add two days.

  4. Day 9

    Bisque fire

    A first, gentle firing to 950 °C. The clay stops being clay and becomes porous and ready to take glaze.

  5. Day 10

    Glaze

    Dipped by hand for a three-second count. We mix all six glazes ourselves and keep a test tile from every bucket.

  6. Days 11–13

    Glaze fire

    Eleven hours up to 1260 °C, a short hold, then a day and a half of cooling with the door shut.
